Total σ per isotope, built coherently from the collision matrix σtot=2πk2∑JgJ∑c(1−ReUcc) — not a sum of independent terms (that is single-level SLBW). Interference asymmetry scales with Γn/Γtot; scattering resonances can rise above the baseline. Removal is by total σ, but forward scatter into the detector makes the effective removal < σ_tot → prefer capture-dominated resonances for quantitative n·d.

Regime physics

⚑ Saturation & the curve of growth

As (n·d)·σ grows the dip deepens toward T≈0 (never exactly zero) and — at fixed temperature — widens. So the width does not encode temperature alone.

Both dips at the same temperature. Raising areal density widens then saturates the dip — so FWHM ⇏ temperature; n·d and T fit jointly. "FWHM gives T without density" holds only optically-thin (τ₀ ≲ 0.3) with Γ and resolution known.

A saturated core is not pure background — it carries resolution-leakage + in-scatter + gammas + frame overlap. A model-assisted constraint, not a free zero.

Degeneracies — why fits are coupled

n·d ⊗ T — jointly encoded (curve of growth); fit together. "Pin one" valid only at τ₀ ≲ 0.3.
resolution ⊗ T — both broaden; calibration determines resolution, carried as a prior.
energy scale (L, t₀) ⊗ resonance position — anchored by the known calibrant.
n·d ⊗ normalization ⊗ background — depth vs scale vs baseline trade off.
n ⊗ d — transmission sees only the product (areal); volumetric needs geometry.

Instrument carried as a correlated calibration prior or fit jointly — not hard-pinned. Hard-pinning reports Ccond; the honest covariance is Cmarg=Ccond+JΣcalJT⪰Ccond. In a toy MC the pinned 1σ bar covered only 20.4% vs the nominal 68.3% (≈4× too small; ~27× near the T–resolution degeneracy). Joint fitting is honest and tighter — sharp resonances re-measure resolution and energy scale.
