Regime physics

⚑ Saturation & the curve of growth

As (n·d)·σ grows the dip deepens toward T≈0 (never exactly zero) and — at fixed temperature — widens. So the width does not encode temperature alone.

Both dips at the same temperature. Raising areal density widens then saturates the dip — so FWHM ⇏ temperature; n·d and T fit jointly. "FWHM gives T without density" holds only optically-thin (τ₀ ≲ 0.3) with Γ and resolution known. Note the distinction: that threshold applies to width-only inversion; under full forward-model fitting opacity is deterministic and modelable, so τ₀ ~ 1 is usable (see the calibration section).

A saturated core is not pure background — it carries resolution-leakage + in-scatter + gammas + frame overlap. A model-assisted constraint, not a free zero.

Degeneracies — why fits are coupled

n·d ⊗ T — jointly encoded (curve of growth); fit together. "Pin one" valid only at τ₀ ≲ 0.3.
resolution ⊗ T — both broaden; calibration determines resolution, carried as a prior.
energy scale (L, t₀) ⊗ resonance position — anchored by the known calibrant.
n·d ⊗ normalization ⊗ background — depth vs scale vs baseline trade off.
n ⊗ d — transmission sees only the product (areal); volumetric needs geometry.

Calibration is the instance where nuclear data and sample state are known and the instrument is solved. Its value comes from which data feature identifies which parameter — and from the degeneracies that fix the order of operations.

Degeneracy structure of the timing model (exact)

Writing the arrival time as t(E)=t0+L/v(E)+lag(E), the fit spans only {1,E−1/2}. Therefore:

constant lag ≡ t₀ — exactly degenerate (residual 3×10⁻¹⁶ µs). ⚠ corrected: a wide energy span does NOT cure this one, contrary to the original claim.
lag ∝ E−1/2 ≡ L — exactly degenerate (residual 4×10⁻¹⁷ µs). So an emission delay of this form is indistinguishable from a longer flight path.
other energy dependences — separable only if the residual after projection exceeds centroid precision (a 1 µs @10 eV lag ∝ E−1 leaves 0.14 µs).
a fixed-time lag is not a fixed extra path — the implied path varies by ~6.3× across 5–200 eV, so the two cannot be conflated.

Consequences. L, t₀ and the kernel location must be fitted jointly under an explicitly stated anchoring convention — not in sequence. The fitted L is an effective flight path that absorbs the emission depth: it is not the surveyed distance, so a discrepancy against the survey is expected and physical, not a validation failure. Because a constant delay is exactly absorbed by t₀, there is no internal check on t₀ — which trigger defines it must be stated externally.

Calibrant & resonance selection (⚠ corrected — the original τ₀ ≲ 0.3 rule was too strict)

Span the band (lever arm for L vs t₀, and the kernel's energy dependence) · use a range of optical depths, favouring weak-to-moderate τ₀ up to ~1 for the resolution core · keep saturated lines for wing/background diagnostics · isolated lines only (no blends) · fit modelled dip shapes, never raw centroids (an asymmetric kernel plus opacity bias the centroid, which biases both L and t₀).

Why the change: at τ₀ = 0.3 the dip is only 26% deep — too weak to build a resolution determination on — while opacity broadening at τ₀ = 1 is only +28% and is deterministic under the forward model, i.e. modelable rather than corrupting. Apparent/natural HWHM ratio: 1.08 / 1.28 / 1.91 / 3.66 at τ₀ = 0.3 / 1 / 3 / 10. Also do not impose a capture-dominance cut — it discards the most resolution-sensitive lines; model the interference instead.

Reality check (new). A real Ta foil is already saturated at its strong low-energy lines: at the 4.28 eV resonance, 25 µm → τ₀ ≈ 2.2 (dip 89% deep) and 100 µm → τ₀ ≈ 8.7. So "use only optically-thin resonances" is not satisfiable there — opacity must be modelled, not avoided.

Is calibration per-pixel? (geometry no, timing maybe)

Geometric per-pixel flight path is unnecessary at VENUS. ΔL/L≈r2/2L02: at L₀ = 25 m, r = 2 cm gives ΔE/E ≈ 6×10⁻⁷; even a 20 cm-square detector's corner gives 3×10⁻⁵ — two to three orders below any realistic resolution. This removes a parameter from the model.

⚠ but the corollary is not that the field collapses to one calibration: a per-pixel/per-chip t₀ (time-walk, clock distribution, cluster-timing estimator) reaches ΔE/E ≈ 1.4×10⁻³ at 10 eV and 6.3×10⁻³ at 200 eV for a 400 ns offset — at or above resolution. So a genuine 1D-ROI-vs-2D calibration disagreement would indicate timing, statistics, or sub-pixel heterogeneity — not geometry.

What calibration must emit

Not point values. The output object is the joint posterior: parameter vector + full covariance (or samples/likelihood if non-Gaussian), plus the resolution model form and its anchoring convention, the validity energy range, the calibrant assumptions it is conditional on, and any correlations with nuclear data.

Why the full covariance is mandatory: discarding the off-diagonal is wrong in both directions. At ρ = −0.861, the variance of one linear combination is overstated 7.2× while the orthogonal one is understated ~1.9×; at ρ = −0.98 the overstatement reaches 50×. Which error you make depends on which combination the downstream observable happens to be sensitive to — so marginal error bars cannot be salvaged.

Confirmed gaps & residual couplings (physics still to settle — not code)

  • Bound-atom lattice dynamics vs free-gas T_eff — trades directly against instrumental width, worst below ~20–40 eV where Doppler dominates.
  • Multiple scattering / accepted in-scatter — fills dips and creates apparent resolution wings; not a scalar background.
  • Detector dead time, MCP gain depletion, pileup, gamma-flash recovery — distorts exactly the short-TOF high-energy lines that carry the resolution information.
  • Frame overlap / wraparound — 5–200 eV arrives at 0.13–0.81 ms; slower neutrons from the previous 16.7 ms frame can alias in.
  • Background needs distinct spectral templates (constant-in-TOF, ~1/E, prompt tail), not a single (B_s, B_o) pair — this is what refutes the nuisance claim above.
  • t₀'s definition — accelerator trigger vs proton-on-target vs moderator reference vs detector trigger vs chopper phase; these drift independently and a constant offset is exactly degenerate with t₀.
  • ENDF resonance-parameter covariance — treating nuclear data as exact converts nuclear-data error into a spurious instrument correction.
  • Dip-location bias under an asymmetric, non-stationary kernel — minimum ≠ centroid ≠ fitted energy once opacity and asymmetry act.
  • Convolution order — Doppler inside σ before exponentiation; instrument kernel after, on Φ·ε·T, with the open beam propagated separately.
  • Calibrant assay — Ta-182 activation, Ta-180m, oxide layers, impurities, thickness map: "known n·d" is itself uncertain.
  • Temperature gradients along the beam (⚠ review round 1) — the measured spectrum is a path mixture of exponentials at different T, not a single cross-section at the mean temperature.
  • Monitor / normalization covariance (⚠ review round 1) — Q_s, Q_o are noisy observations sharing monitors, not exact divisors; run-to-run drift correlates the arms.
  • Flux-weighted ROI averaging (⚠ review round 1) — the pixel/ROI average is illumination- and energy-weighted and entangled with the detector PSF, not a plain area mean.
  • Thermal strain, creep, foil bowing (⚠ review round 1, in-situ) — geometry changes that are not isotropic expansion and break the deterministic n·d(T) link.
  • The design metric itself (⚠ review round 1) — identifiability and experimental design rest on the joint Fisher information over {n·d, T, L, t₀, θ_res, backgrounds, nuclear nuisances}, not on width ratios alone; the width-ratio arguments in this document are the physical intuition behind that computation, not its substitute.

The unifying result: optical depth τ₀ governs both separations

In the optically thin limit the two observables decouple cleanly:

AREA carries n·d, free of T. Doppler conserves the cross-section area, so the equivalent width measures n·d independently of temperature: d ln W / d ln T = 0.008 at τ₀ ≈ 0.1.
WIDTH carries T, free of n·d. The Doppler width ∝ √T sets the shape while the depth scale is set by n·d (the earlier FWHM result, valid at τ₀ ≲ 0.3).

Saturation destroys both separations at once — and continuously. ⚠ review round 1: corrections begin at O(τ₀); there is no sharp threshold where both separations fail together. For a representative fixed-Γ line (Voigt, Γ = 60 meV, Ta-181-like at 10 eV) temperature leaks into the area as d ln W/d ln T = 0.023 → 0.063 → 0.110 at τ₀ ≈ 0.3 → 1 → 3 (the earlier labels 0.37/1.2/3.7 were the 150 K peak depths — corrected to 300 K), while opacity broadening inflates the width by +18.6% from τ₀ 0.3 → 1 for a Lorentzian shape (a Gaussian gives +12.1%). These numbers are line-specific, not functions of τ₀ alone: a pure conserved-area Gaussian scale family reaches 0.021 → 0.061 → 0.168 → 0.327 over the same range, and the fixed-Γ Voigt is non-monotonic — falling back to 0.092 by τ₀ ≈ 10, because deep saturation moves the equivalent width onto the T-independent Lorentzian wings. τ₀ remains the master design variable — it controls the sensitivities — but any threshold quoted on it is an illustration for a stated line shape, never a universal constant.

Method note (honest): my first numerical test of this normalised the line profile to unit peak, which silently holds τ₀ fixed instead of n·d and manufactured a spurious T-dependence in the thin limit. The correct test holds n·d fixed with an area-normalised cross-section. Recording the error because it is exactly the kind of mistake that would otherwise have been "confirmed" by a plot.

Density instance — known isotope + T → n·d

Identified from: the equivalent width / integrated dip strength — not the depth alone, since depth trades directly against normalization and background.

Optimal sample thickness is a solved problem — under stated conditions. With Poisson counting, Var(τ)=(eτ+1)/N, so minimising the relative error on τ gives eτ(τ−2)=2 ⟹ τ₀ ≈ 2.22. Penalty for being off: 3.6× worse at τ₀ = 0.3, 4.7× worse at τ₀ = 8. ⚠ review round 1 — this is an asymptotic, equal-exposure, known-normalization result: backgrounds, monitors and unequal open/sample allocation shift the optimum, and the log-ratio estimator itself degrades at extreme depth (at τ₀ = 8, ten expected sample counts already need N ≈ 3×10⁴ open counts/bin).

⚑ A real design tension this exposes: the density optimum (τ₀ ≈ 2.2) and the resolution-calibration optimum (τ₀ ≲ 1) are different. The sample and the calibrant genuinely want different thicknesses — they are not interchangeable specimens. (Neither optimum is universal — both depend on flux, background, binning and kernel — but the tension between them is generic.)

May T be pinned? ⚠ revised (review round 1) — not by a τ₀ branch alone. The operational rule: pinning is admissible only when marginalizing over T's external uncertainty shifts the n·d posterior by less than a stated fraction of the total error budget; and a T prior is always preferred over a hard pin. The thin regime is why the test passes at small τ₀ (area conservation makes the sensitivity vanish) — but the test, not the regime label, is the gate.

degeneracies: n·d ⊗ normalization ⊗ background · n·d ⊗ T (curve of growth) · per-isotope n·d ⊗ each other wherever lines overlap

Temperature instance — known n·d → T_eff

Identified from: the full line shape (never the raw FWHM — established earlier), specifically the Doppler contribution in excess of the pinned instrument kernel.

⚑ Where the temperature information lives. The relative Doppler width ΔD/E∝E−1/2 falls with energy, while a moderator-dominated instrument gives a roughly constant relative resolution (see the resolution section). So the Doppler-to-instrument width ratio improves toward low energy — temperature fits should favour the lowest usable resonances. ⚠ review round 1: the width ratio is a proxy, not the decision metric — actual information also carries resonance strength, natural width, opacity, flux, background and post-convolution derivatives. The canonical selection ranks candidate resonance sets by expected information on T_eff after marginalizing density, kernel, background and nuclear-data uncertainty; under moderator-dominated resolution that ranking lands on the low lines, which is why the rule of thumb survives.

⚠ Correction to an earlier version of this section. I first compared Doppler against a fixed timing uncertainty, which makes relative resolution grow as √E and produced a "crossover at 22 eV / 7 eV". That model is unphysical — the dominant term is the moderator emission time, which scales as 1/√E and therefore gives constant ΔE/E. The crossover figures are withdrawn. With realistic moderator-dominated resolution (ΔE/E ≈ 1.05×10⁻² — an assumed moderator-scaling figure, not a measured VENUS kernel; the measured kernel is an open call below) the instrument width equals or exceeds the Doppler width across essentially the whole band, crossing only near the very bottom (≈ 5.2 eV): the ratio is 1.02 at 5 eV, 0.72 at 10 eV, 0.23 at 100 eV. The directional conclusion (favour low-E lines) survives; the numbers did not.

Consequence — and it is a serious one. Temperature is extracted from a broadening contribution that is sub-dominant over nearly the whole band, and at best comparable at its very bottom (ratio ≈ 1 near 5 eV — ⚠ "everywhere" was an overstatement, corrected in review round 1). That is exactly why the resolution must be pinned from a calibrant and propagated with its covariance: a small θ_res error becomes a large T error. It also independently explains the earlier finding that VENUS suppresses Doppler sensitivity by roughly 4×.

May n·d be pinned? ⚠ revised (review round 1) — same rule as pinning T: only when marginalizing n·d's external uncertainty moves the T posterior by less than a stated fraction of the error budget; prefer a prior over a pin. The danger it guards: since T ∝ Δ_D², a fractional width error ε maps to (1+ε)²−1 in temperature — an unmodelled τ₀ drift from 0.3 → 1 inflates the apparent temperature by +41% ≈ +122 K at 300 K; 1 → 3 gives +124% ≈ +371 K (exact values; the first-order 2ε rule reported earlier — +112 K / +297 K — understates the bias, badly at large ε). If the fitted width contains instrument or natural components removed in quadrature, the bias amplifies further. Opacity masquerades as heat.

degeneracies: T ⊗ θ_res (the reason calibration runs first) · T ⊗ n·d (curve of growth) · T ⊗ energy scale (a stretched axis mimics broadening)

In-situ instance — neither known → n·d + T, tracked

What makes separation possible at all. Not the shape of a single line. Two independent levers:

(1) Energy dependence across resonances. Temperature acts coherently on every line via Δ_D ∝ √E, whereas optical depth acts through each line's own σ_peak. Different functional signatures ⟹ typically separable with ≥2 resonances of differing strength spanning energy. ⚠ review round 1: that count is a rule of thumb — neither necessary nor sufficient. One well-resolved line can suffice in favourable conditions; many lines stay degenerate if the kernel, t₀, backgrounds or T_eff are unconstrained. The identifiability screen, not the line count, is the gate.
(2) Global fit across the thermal series. n·d is shared across frames (absent mass change) while T varies per frame — a shared-parameter global fit, which is a large identifiability gain over frame-by-frame fitting.

⚑ Thermal expansion is a first-class term, not a correction. For a free-standing foil the areal density falls as (1+αΔT)−2: over 1000 K that is −1.25% (Ta), −0.89% (W), −2.78% (Au). If left out of the model it masquerades as mass loss or composition change — precisely the quantity an in-situ experiment claims to measure. ⚠ review round 1 — validity conditions: those percentages are constant-α illustrations; over ranges like 1000 K use the integrated form ndj=nd0·exp(−2∫α(T)dT), and the free-isotropic law breaks under fixtures (anisotropic strain), phase changes, or near melting (Au: 1337 K) — then an integrated strain model with uncertainty replaces it. The shared quantity across the series is the reference n·d₀, with per-frame n·dⱼ following the deterministic link — not a literally constant n·d.

Composition evolution constrains what may be shared: if composition genuinely changes (phase change, diffusion), per-isotope n·d cannot be shared across the series. The model must declare which parameters are global and which are per-frame — that declaration is part of the experiment definition, not an implementation choice.

Two statistical traps that silently bias results

Neyman χ² bias. Taking the per-bin variance from the data (σ² = N_observed) generally biases fitted amplitudes low — deep bins get over-weighted. ⚠ review round 1: the sign is not universal once backgrounds, zero-bin prescriptions and nonlinear shape parameters enter — the Poisson likelihood is the defensible default; Pearson χ² (variance from the model) is the minimum.
Per-pixel UQ underestimates. ⚠ re-badged (review round 1): the ~3–4× understatement of per-superpixel scatter is an empirical VENUS observation, not a general statistical fact — under regular high-count conditions the inverse-Hessian covariance is asymptotically correct, so a shortfall this size points at model mismatch, ignored calibration covariance, or spatial correlation cause unattributed. Bootstrap or posterior sampling is honest but per-pixel MCMC over ~10⁵ pixels is intractable → reserve it for ROIs / a global co-fit, and report a χ²-scaled covariance elsewhere with the scaling disclosed.

The joint-fit contract. The instrument enters as a correlated prior (or is fitted jointly), never hard-pinned; the reported covariance is Cmarg=Ccond+JΣcalJT. Nuclear-data covariance propagates the same way — otherwise evaluation error is silently reclassified as a physical result.

The spatial dimension — 1D ROI · 2D per-pixel · 3D

⚑ 1D-ROI and 2D-per-pixel disagree by a predictable, signed amount

Because exp is convex, Jensen's inequality gives ⟨exp(−τ)⟩≥exp(−⟨τ⟩). Fitting an aggregated ROI with a uniform model therefore returns τfit≈⟨τ⟩−Var(τ)/2 — so 1D-ROI systematically UNDERESTIMATES areal density whenever the ROI is heterogeneous. Verified numerically (prediction vs Monte-Carlo agree): at ⟨τ⟩ = 2, a thickness spread of 10 % → −1.0%, 20 % → −4.1%, 30 % → −9.0% in n·d. ⚠ review round 1: −Var(τ)/2 is the leading cumulant only (the expansion continues +κ₃/6 − …) and the exact offset is distribution-dependent — at 30% spread it ranges −8.5% (two-point) to −8.7% (uniform) to −9.0% (Gaussian).

Consequence for the never-tested 1D↔2D check: it is not "they should agree" — the offset has a predicted value: exactly ⟨e−τ⟩ computed from the fitted 2D field (all cumulants, with illumination weighting), read as −Var(τ)/2 to leading order. That converts a vague consistency hope into a quantitative test. Counts may legitimately be summed over an ROI (Poisson sums are Poisson), but the ROI forward model must then be ⟨exp(−τ)⟩, not exp(−⟨τ⟩).

The estimator axis, and when regularization is physically justified

independent per-pixel → adaptive binning → spatial regularization → joint spatial-spectral inversion. Regularization is justified when: (a) the underlying field really is smooth or piecewise-smooth on the pixel/PSF scale — use edge-preserving (TV-like) priors where the sample has genuine boundaries, not Gaussian smoothing; and (b) the detector PSF already correlates neighbouring pixels, so independent per-pixel fitting is statistically inefficient, not merely unsmoothed. It is not justified as cosmetic noise suppression: regularization trades variance for bias, so the reported uncertainty must account for the effective degrees of freedom it consumes.

⚑ Why 3D is a different problem — and when it factorises

Each measurement is a line integral: τ(E,ray)=∑i∫ni(x)σi(E;T(x))dl.

Uniform T → it FACTORISES. σ leaves the integral, so τ(E,ray) is linear in the line integral of n. Correct order: reconstruct the linear quantity τ tomographically, then fit per voxel.
Spatially varying T → it does NOT factorise. σ depends on position, so reconstruction and spectral fitting must be solved jointly. This is why in-situ 3D (thermal gradients) is fundamentally harder than density-only 3D.

Reconstruction operates on line integrals — under two ⚠ conditions (review round 1). (i) The linearity of τ=−lnT holds for the model-domain transmission: once expected counts are convolved in TOF, −ln(counts ratio) per energy bin is not a linear Radon sinogram — deconvolve, or fit a count-domain forward model. (ii) The earlier absolute "never reconstruct fitted densities" is softened to a criterion: back-projecting per-ray fitted values is legitimate iff the fitted quantity is a line integral of a common scalar density (uniform or known T); it is invalid when the ray fit mixes temperature, composition, resolution or opacity into something non-additive along the ray.

Results, QC & uncertainty — where every instance converges

Every experiment terminates in the same product family, so this is shared scaffolding, not per-experiment reporting: value map · 1σ uncertainty map · goodness-of-fit map · convergence/trust mask · residual cube.

⚑ A correlated systematic does not average down

The calibration-covariance contribution is spatially correlated — it biases the whole field coherently — so the variance of an N-pixel mean is σstat2/N+σsys2, and binning cannot beat the second term. With 5% per-pixel statistics and a 1% correlated floor: N = 100 → 1.12%, N = 10⁴ → 1.001%, N = 10⁵ → 1.000%. Reporting a spatial average with a 1/√N error bar is therefore wrong by construction. ⚠ review round 1 — scope: this hard floor is exact for the calibration term, which is common-mode by construction (one shared parameter vector biases every pixel coherently, ρ = 1). Systematics with finite spatial correlation soften to σsys2(1+(N−1)ρ)/N and partially average down.

Consistency tests — each with a predicted value, not a hope

1D ↔ 2D — predicted offset ⟨e^(−τ)⟩ from the fitted 2D field (−Var(τ)/2 to leading order — see above), not zero. Agreement at zero would itself indicate a homogeneous ROI or a masked bug.
transmission ↔ counts — should agree within the ratio-bias (≈1/λ_o) and the resolution-order bias; a larger disagreement localises to flux or background modelling.
deviance — Poisson deviance against its calibrated reference (parametric bootstrap / posterior predictive — D/(n−k) → 1 only at high counts, ⚠ review round 1); a systematic departure is model inadequacy, not noise.
per-resonance residuals — must be reported per line. A model wrong in one resonance but averaged away globally is the failure mode this catches.

Model adequacy must stay visible. Nuisance flexibility has to be restricted enough that unexplained structure cannot be absorbed into normalization or background. If the background can reproduce any residual, the fit can never report that the physics is wrong — which is the single most dangerous property an analysis can have.

The resolution function — what it physically is

Why this section exists: it was missing from the first pass of this layer — resolution appeared only as a chain step and as a calibration fit target, i.e. as a black box inside a whitebox. That was a scope gap, and closing it immediately produced a correction to the temperature section above. Derived and numerically verified here.

⚑ The moderator emission delay is an extra flight path

A neutron detected at energy E was born fast and had to slow down inside the moderator before leaking out. The slowing-down time to reach E scales as τmod=C/E. Convert that delay to an equivalent path: ΔL=v·τmod=2E/m·C/E=C2/m — energy-independent.

So a 1/√E emission delay is exactly a constant flight-path offset. This is the physical origin of the exact L-degeneracy found in the calibration section, and it is large: a 3 µs delay at 10 eV corresponds to 13.1 cm of apparent extra path — 0.53% of a 25 m flight path (1 µs → 4.4 cm; 10 µs → 43.7 cm). Since a 0.3% L error is already catastrophic for the fit, the moderator delay is not a refinement — it is the dominant reason the fitted L must differ from the surveyed distance, and why it is meaningless to "check" one against the other.

Components, and how each scales

Independent timing contributions convolve (widths add in quadrature only where each is Gaussian — the moderator term is not, so quadrature is a width approximation only). With ΔE/E=2Δt/t and t∝E−1/2:

This is the fact that corrected the temperature section: relative resolution does not grow across the band, so the earlier Doppler-vs-instrument "crossover" was an artefact.

Constraints any admissible kernel must satisfy — and why a Gaussian fails

Non-negative: K ≥ 0 (it is a probability density over arrival times).
Normalised: ∫K dt = 1 — the kernel redistributes neutrons in time, it must not create or destroy them.
Causal: K = 0 before emission. Nothing arrives before it left.
Position-anchored: one convention (mode or mean at zero) must be fixed, or the kernel's location fights t₀ and L.

A Gaussian violates causality: it has support at negative times. The leaked fraction is Φ(−μ/σ) — 15.9% at μ = σ, 2.3% at 2σ, 0.14% at 3σ. So a Gaussian is only defensible where the mean delay exceeds ~3–4σ. The moderator term is both wide and skewed, which is precisely the regime where a symmetric Gaussian is least valid. Ikeda–Carpenter is causal by construction (its t²e^(−αt) slowing-down term vanishes at t = 0), which is the physics reason to prefer it over a Gaussian — not merely a better fit.

Ikeda–Carpenter parameters, physically: α = slowing-down decay rate (energy-dependent, faster neutrons escape sooner); β = storage/thermal decay rate; R = the fraction emitted through the storage channel. A tabulated (UDR) kernel is the alternative — it captures real geometry, filters and collimation that no analytic form knows about, but it is only as good as its sampling and does not extrapolate. Physics fixes the family (positive, normalised, causal, moderator-skewed); the choice within it is a modelling decision.

Why asymmetry biases the energy scale — "fit shapes, not centroids" is physics

⚠ corrected (review round 1) — the earlier claim that these shifts "tilt the L–t₀ line, biasing both parameters" was wrong, and contradicted this document's own degeneracy structure. The moderator tail runs to late times, displacing every observed dip centroid; left uncorrected, a mean-vs-mode offset d shifts apparent energy by −2d/t: for d = 2 µs that is −0.50% at 5 eV, −3.13% at 200 eV. But inside the calibration fit a constant offset is absorbed exactly into t₀ (verified: refit residual 10⁻¹³ µs, L untouched) and an E−1/2 component exactly into L — only the part of the kernel's centroid drift outside span{1, E−1/2} survives projection and genuinely distorts the fit. Why "fit modelled dip shapes, never raw centroids" still stands: (i) the Ikeda–Carpenter asymmetry drifts with energy in exactly such a non-absorbable way; (ii) opacity displaces each line's centroid by a depth-dependent amount — not a timing function at all, so it corrupts a centroid fit line-by-line; (iii) silently absorbed offsets redefine L and t₀ away from the declared kernel anchor, breaking the calibration transfer to sample fits with different line sets. Shape fitting models all three by construction. (A centroid scheme is self-consistent only if the kernel mean is the declared anchor, used identically everywhere — fragile, and still opacity-biased.)

Shared stage · Qualification (⚠ added in review round 1 — runs before any reduction, fail-closed)

Run & beam qualification

Pulse IDs, live time, proton charge / beam-monitor records, chopper state, trigger/timing epochs, run completeness, sample motion, frame-overlap condition, metadata consistency. A corrupt or truncated run fails here — before it can be mixed with a valid open beam.

Detector response & rate validity

Dead time, pile-up, gain depletion, nonlinearity: characterized, corrected with propagated uncertainty, and bounded by a hard rate-validity limit. Rates differ between sample and open runs, so these effects never cancel in a ratio — and uncorrected losses invalidate the Poisson likelihood itself.

Background measurements

Dedicated background data are first-class inputs, not nuisance guesses: blocked-beam runs and black-resonance (notch) filters constrain the additive components per spectral template (constant-in-TOF · ~1/E · prompt tail).

Reduction never rebins to energy. Nuclear-covariance policy (⚠ review round 1)

"Covariance carried" has a failure policy: availability checked per library/version; non-PSD matrices repaired and disclosed; missing covariance → a declared default uncertainty or rejection, never silent zero. Attenuate + instrument

Beer-Lambert with per-instance n·d structure; E(t;L,t0) evaluated here on the native-TOF bins; resolution kernel K(t∣E) applied to expected counts (Φ·ε·T), position-anchored. ⚠ review round 1 — completeness policy: every non-Beer-Lambert contribution (container/environment attenuation, scatter-in, multiple scattering, PSF mixing, self-shielding) is modelled, bounded with a stated magnitude, or measured — "visible as model inadequacy" is not a policy for a predictable physical term.

Objective

Counts arm: Poisson/KL deviance, with the GOF reference calibrated (bootstrap / posterior predictive — D/(n−k)→1 only at high counts, ⚠ review round 1). Transmission arm: Gaussian χ² with model-based (Pearson) weights — never Neyman (data-based variance generally biases amplitudes low).

◇ Identifiability screen — layered (⚠ review round 1 — was "once per configuration")

(1) Design time — expected information for the planned exposure. Calibrant foil selection — Ta / W / Au / Ag

A calibrant serves two distinct jobs, and they favour different foils — which is why "cleanest resonance" and "most preferred" need not be the same material.

Job A · energy scale (L, t₀) — wants many accurately-known, well-separated resonance positions spanning the widest E−1/2 range. Multi-isotope is harmless: a position is a position.
Job B · resolution function θ_res(E) — wants the instrumental width not swamped by intrinsic Doppler+natural width, sampled across the band, at unsaturated optical depth, on isolated lines. Favours heavy nuclei (ΔD∝1/A).

Two thicknesses of the same foil verified — "a linear combination of measurements with two thicknesses… yields better energy resolution than either foil alone… no penalty in counting statistics." Why W is preferred — physics-grounded rationale (adopted)

The rationale rests on solid nuclear physics: the even-even isotopes W-182/184/186 have spin I = 0, so each contributes only a single s-wave spin group (J = ½) and therefore a sparse, widely-spaced ladder. Several sparse ladders superimposed give band coverage with isolated, non-blended lines — whereas one dense ladder (Ta, I = 7/2) crowds and blends. Combined with refractory robustness and short-lived activation, this explains the preference. physics-grounded not literature-attributed

Adopted as the working justification by project decision, in preference to a generic practitioner answer. Note honestly what this is and is not: the underlying nuclear physics is certain (even-even nuclei always have I = 0 ground state, hence a single s-wave spin group), but no published source was found that states this as the reason practitioners choose W. It is a derived explanation, not a cited one.

Corroborating but not decisive: a dedicated study exists on Neutron Doppler broadening studies of tantalum and tungsten metal — Ta and W are the canonical pair for this class of work.
