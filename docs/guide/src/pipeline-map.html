Regime physics

⚑ Saturation & the curve of growth

As (n·d)·σ grows the dip deepens toward T≈0 (never exactly zero) and — at fixed temperature — widens. So the width does not encode temperature alone.

Both dips at the same temperature. Raising areal density widens then saturates the dip — so FWHM ⇏ temperature; n·d and T fit jointly. "FWHM gives T without density" holds only optically-thin (τ₀ ≲ 0.3) with Γ and resolution known. Note the distinction: that threshold applies to width-only inversion; under full forward-model fitting opacity is deterministic and modelable, so τ₀ ~ 1 is usable (see the calibration section).

A saturated core is not pure background — it carries resolution-leakage + in-scatter + gammas + frame overlap. A model-assisted constraint, not a free zero.

Degeneracies — why fits are coupled

n·d ⊗ T — jointly encoded (curve of growth); fit together. "Pin one" valid only at τ₀ ≲ 0.3.
resolution ⊗ T — both broaden; calibration determines resolution, carried as a prior.
energy scale (L, t₀) ⊗ resonance position — anchored by the known calibrant.
n·d ⊗ normalization ⊗ background — depth vs scale vs baseline trade off.
n ⊗ d — transmission sees only the product (areal); volumetric needs geometry.

Calibration is the instance where nuclear data and sample state are known and the instrument is solved. Its value comes from which data feature identifies which parameter — and from the degeneracies that fix the order of operations.

Degeneracy structure of the timing model (exact)

Writing the arrival time as t(E)=t0+L/v(E)+lag(E), the fit spans only {1,E−1/2}. Therefore:

constant lag ≡ t₀ — exactly degenerate (residual 3×10⁻¹⁶ µs). ⚠ corrected: a wide energy span does NOT cure this one, contrary to the original claim.
lag ∝ E−1/2 ≡ L — exactly degenerate (residual 4×10⁻¹⁷ µs). So an emission delay of this form is indistinguishable from a longer flight path.
other energy dependences — separable only if the residual after projection exceeds centroid precision (a 1 µs @10 eV lag ∝ E−1 leaves 0.14 µs).
a fixed-time lag is not a fixed extra path — the implied path varies by ~6.3× across 5–200 eV, so the two cannot be conflated.

Consequences. L, t₀ and the kernel location must be fitted jointly under an explicitly stated anchoring convention — not in sequence. The fitted L is an effective flight path that absorbs the emission depth: it is not the surveyed distance, so a discrepancy against the survey is expected and physical, not a validation failure. Because a constant delay is exactly absorbed by t₀, there is no internal check on t₀ — which trigger defines it must be stated externally.

Calibrant & resonance selection (⚠ corrected — the original τ₀ ≲ 0.3 rule was too strict)

Span the band (lever arm for L vs t₀, and the kernel's energy dependence) · use a range of optical depths, favouring weak-to-moderate τ₀ up to ~1 for the resolution core · keep saturated lines for wing/background diagnostics · isolated lines only (no blends) · fit modelled dip shapes, never raw centroids (an asymmetric kernel plus opacity bias the centroid, which biases both L and t₀).

Why the change: at τ₀ = 0.3 the dip is only 26% deep — too weak to build a resolution determination on — while opacity broadening at τ₀ = 1 is only +28% and is deterministic under the forward model, i.e. modelable rather than corrupting. Apparent/natural HWHM ratio: 1.08 / 1.28 / 1.91 / 3.66 at τ₀ = 0.3 / 1 / 3 / 10. Also do not impose a capture-dominance cut — it discards the most resolution-sensitive lines; model the interference instead.

Reality check (new). A real Ta foil is already saturated at its strong low-energy lines: at the 4.28 eV resonance, 25 µm → τ₀ ≈ 2.2 (dip 89% deep) and 100 µm → τ₀ ≈ 8.7. So "use only optically-thin resonances" is not satisfiable there — opacity must be modelled, not avoided.

Is calibration per-pixel? (geometry no, timing maybe)

Geometric per-pixel flight path is unnecessary at VENUS. ΔL/L≈r2/2L02: at L₀ = 25 m, r = 2 cm gives ΔE/E ≈ 6×10⁻⁷; even a 20 cm-square detector's corner gives 3×10⁻⁵ — two to three orders below any realistic resolution. This removes a parameter from the model.

⚠ but the corollary is not that the field collapses to one calibration: a per-pixel/per-chip t₀ (time-walk, clock distribution, cluster-timing estimator) reaches ΔE/E ≈ 1.4×10⁻³ at 10 eV and 6.3×10⁻³ at 200 eV for a 400 ns offset — at or above resolution. So a genuine 1D-ROI-vs-2D calibration disagreement would indicate timing, statistics, or sub-pixel heterogeneity — not geometry.

What calibration must emit

Not point values. The output object is the joint posterior: parameter vector + full covariance (or samples/likelihood if non-Gaussian), plus the resolution model form and its anchoring convention, the validity energy range, the calibrant assumptions it is conditional on, and any correlations with nuclear data.

Why the full covariance is mandatory: discarding the off-diagonal is wrong in both directions. At ρ = −0.861, the variance of one linear combination is overstated 7.2× while the orthogonal one is understated ~1.9×; at ρ = −0.98 the overstatement reaches 50×. Which error you make depends on which combination the downstream observable happens to be sensitive to — so marginal error bars cannot be salvaged.

Confirmed gaps & residual couplings (physics still to settle — not code)

  • Bound-atom lattice dynamics vs free-gas T_eff — trades directly against instrumental width, worst below ~20–40 eV where Doppler dominates.
  • Multiple scattering / accepted in-scatter — fills dips and creates apparent resolution wings; not a scalar background.
  • Detector dead time, MCP gain depletion, pileup, gamma-flash recovery — distorts exactly the short-TOF high-energy lines that carry the resolution information.
  • Frame overlap / wraparound — 5–200 eV arrives at 0.13–0.81 ms; slower neutrons from the previous 16.7 ms frame can alias in.
  • Background needs distinct spectral templates (constant-in-TOF, ~1/E, prompt tail), not a single (B_s, B_o) pair — this is what refutes the nuisance claim above.
  • t₀'s definition — accelerator trigger vs proton-on-target vs moderator reference vs detector trigger vs chopper phase; these drift independently and a constant offset is exactly degenerate with t₀.
  • ENDF resonance-parameter covariance — treating nuclear data as exact converts nuclear-data error into a spurious instrument correction.
  • Dip-location bias under an asymmetric, non-stationary kernel — minimum ≠ centroid ≠ fitted energy once opacity and asymmetry act.
  • Convolution order — Doppler inside σ before exponentiation; instrument kernel after, on Φ·ε·T, with the open beam propagated separately.
  • Calibrant assay — Ta-182 activation, Ta-180m, oxide layers, impurities, thickness map: "known n·d" is itself uncertain.

  • Per-pixel / per-chip timing calibration — does it exist, and what is its magnitude?

Calibrant foil selection — Ta / W / Au / Ag

A calibrant serves two distinct jobs, and they favour different foils — which is why "cleanest resonance" and "most preferred" need not be the same material.

Job A · energy scale (L, t₀) — wants many accurately-known, well-separated resonance positions spanning the widest E−1/2 range. Multi-isotope is harmless: a position is a position.
Job B · resolution function θ_res(E) — wants the instrumental width not swamped by intrinsic Doppler+natural width, sampled across the band, at unsaturated optical depth, on isolated lines. Favours heavy nuclei (ΔD∝1/A).

1W Resolution function. Best kernel coverage — largest E−1/2 sampling gap only 0.132. Strong low-band lines at 4.1 & 7.7 eV verified. Multi-isotope (W-182/183/184/186) → more nuclear-data bookkeeping; harmless for Job A. Refractory, robust, cheap, often already in-beam. Activation W-187 ≈ 24 h — decays away in days t½ unverified.
2Ta Verified flight-path standard verified. Effectively mononuclidic (Ta-181 = 99.988%); dense ladder gives many lines across the band. I = 7/2 → many spin groups → ladder crowds/blends above ~50–100 eV. Refractory. Activation Ta-182 ≈ 114 d — a real handling nuisance vs W t½ unverified.
3Au Energy-scale anchor. Best lever arm (ρ = −0.79). Mononuclidic (100%), inert, international standard → best-known parameters. 4.906 eV line strongly capture-dominated (Γ = 137.5 meV, Γn = 15.0 meV ⇒ Γ_γ ≈ 122 meV) — hence its exceptional cleanliness verified. Kernel coverage — worst sampling gap 0.305 (~2× the others), driven by a large 4.9 → ~46.7 eV hole gap unverified. Soft, expensive. Chemically inert (never oxidises); Au-198 ≈ 2.7 d, decays fast.
4Ag A known-temperature thermometry reference (Ag-109 5.19 eV) — a different job entirely. The only quantitative physics penalty: lightest ⇒ Doppler width ~35% larger, so the instrumental share of the observed width falls to 27% vs ~40% for the heavies. Two comparable isotopes (51.8 / 48.2%) interleave ladders. Tarnishes. Long-lived activation (Ag-110m ≈ 250 d, Ag-108m ≈ 438 y) t½ unverified.

Recommendation — two foils, each in two thicknesses

W (or Ta) as the primary multi-line calibrant for θ_res(E) coverage, plus Au as an independent energy-scale anchor and cross-check. They optimise different jobs, and Au's coverage gap stops mattering once it is not carrying the kernel.

Two thicknesses of the same foil verified — "a linear combination of measurements with two thicknesses… yields better energy resolution than either foil alone… no penalty in counting statistics." This resolves the saturation problem directly: a thin (~10 µm) foil keeps τ₀ ≲ 1 for resolution work while a thicker one supplies deliberately black lines for background/wing diagnosis. All four candidates saturate (τ₀ > 1) by roughly 25–50 µm at their strong low-energy lines, so a single intermediate thickness cannot serve both.

Fit modelled dip shapes, never raw centroids verified — practice is a linear t vs E−1/2 fit for a first L, then refinement by χ² against evaluated data.

Why is W often preferred? — hypothesis, not confirmed

No source was found stating the reason. The proposed explanation rests on solid nuclear physics: the even-even isotopes W-182/184/186 have spin I = 0, so each contributes only a single s-wave spin group (J = ½) and therefore a sparse, widely-spaced ladder. Several sparse ladders superimposed give band coverage with isolated, non-blended lines — whereas one dense ladder (Ta, I = 7/2) crowds. Combined with refractory robustness and short-lived activation, that would explain the preference. hypothesis

Suggestive but not decisive: a dedicated study exists on Neutron Doppler broadening studies of tantalum and tungsten metal — Ta and W are the canonical pair for this class of work.

To settle it: ask whether the stated reason is (a) line isolation, (b) refractory robustness, (c) short activation (W-187 24 h vs Ta-182 114 d), or (d) already present in the beamline.

Open items on the foil choice

  • Does Au-197 really have a 4.9 → ~46.7 eV gap? The Au ranking depends on it more than on anything else. The GELINA study of ¹⁹⁷Au+n below 200 eV is the authoritative source.
  • Activation half-lives — quoted from general knowledge, not verified in session; they carry real operational weight (Ta-182 114 d vs W-187 24 h).
  • The actual reason for W's preference — see above; the instrument team can likely resolve this immediately.
