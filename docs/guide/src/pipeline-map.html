Regime physics — what constrains the fit

⚑ Black resonances & saturation

Where (n·d)·σ ≫ 1, transmission bottoms out at T = 0. The saturated core carries no information — the Fisher information for n·d and T collapses there; all the fit sensitivity lives on the wings.

Unsaturated dip — depth encodes n·d, width encodes T; the fit reads the wings.

Black dip — core is flat zero, blind to more material or more heat. But at T=0 the true signal is exactly zero, so any measured counts there are pure background — a black resonance is a built-in probe of B(E) and the resolution wings. Dual-use, not just a dead zone.

Design consequence: too much material → every strong resonance goes black → only weak resonances stay quantitative. Isotope + energy-window selection is part of the physics, and there is a hard dynamic-range cutoff.

Degeneracies — why fits must be partitioned

resolution ⊗ temperature — both broaden; unfittable together → calibration pins resolution first.
energy scale (L,t₀) ⊗ resonance position — a wrong axis mimics shifted resonances → pinned by the known calibrant.
n·d ⊗ normalization ⊗ background — dip depth vs overall scale vs baseline all trade off.
n ⊗ d — transmission sees only the product (areal); volumetric needs geometry / tomography.
