NEREIDS · VENUS neutron-resonance imaging · forward model

The forward model — nuclear data → the measurement

One deterministic chain. Every fit runs this same model — calibration and the three experiments differ only in which parameters are known vs solved. But the parameters are coupled: density and temperature are jointly encoded in every resonance, and the instrument is never perfectly known — so the honest picture is joint estimation with calibration carried as a constraint, not clean partitions with the rest pinned.

Transformation chain

1Microscopic cross-section σ₀(E)coherent Reich–Moore

Total σ per isotope, built coherently from the collision matrix σ_tot = (2π/k²)Σ_J g_J Σ_c(1−Re U_cc) — not a sum of independent resonance + potential + interference terms (that additive form is single-level SLBW, exact only for one isolated resonance; same-Jπ levels interfere non-additively). Interference asymmetry scales with the elastic branching Γn/Γtot: capture-dominated dips are near-symmetric, scattering-dominated ones can rise above the potential baseline. For scattering resonances, forward scatter into the detector makes effective removal < σ_tot → prefer capture-dominated resonances for quantitative n·d.

2Doppler broadening → σ(E; T)Δ_D ≈ √(4k_B·T_eff·E / A)

Thermal motion broadens σ; temperature enters the whole pipeline only here. Corrected: the exact free-gas kernel conserves the reaction rate (1/v preserved exactly); the σ(E)-area is conserved only to O((Δ_D/E)²), and Δ_D∝√(TE/A) is its high-energy approximation. For lattice-bound solids the relevant temperature is an effective kinetic T_eff from the phonon DOS / Debye model, not the thermodynamic T (a real, scalar correction — ~+1.6% width for Ta, ~+4% for W at 300 K). It is isotropic: crystallographic texture does not affect the resonance width for cubic metals (distinct from the Bragg-edge texture effect).

3Attenuation → transmissionT(E) = exp(−Σᵢ (n·d)ᵢ σᵢ)

Beer–Lambert, areal density in atoms/barn. It is exact for the uncollided beam at any optical depth — strong attenuation is the self-shielding, not a correction to it. The real corrections are: (i) detector acceptance / in-scatter — scattered neutrons re-entering the pixel (likely negligible for the MCP-TPX geometry, to confirm with the instrument team); (ii) sub-pixel heterogeneity — the pixel transmission is ⟨exp(−τ)⟩ ≠ exp(−⟨τ⟩), so thickness variation / porosity biases n·d.

4Energy ↔ time-of-flightE = ½mₙ(L/(t−t₀))²

The measurement lives in TOF; the physics in energy. L varies per pixel off-axis, so the energy axis is per-pixel. The energy-dependent moderator emission delay must live in exactly one of {t₀, the resolution-kernel mean} — never double-counted in both.

5Instrument resolutionK(t │ E) — conditional, energy-dependent

Not a shift-invariant convolution R(t−t′). Because the Ikeda–Carpenter moderator parameters vary with energy, the operator is a non-stationary conditional kernel K(t│E), and it must be position-anchored (its mean lag is degenerate with a flight-path error). Broadens the resonance and is degenerate with Doppler — the reason calibration exists. It should act on the expected counts (Φ·ε·T): convolving transmission alone is exact only for smooth flux — where flux structure meets steep resonance wings it biases the width (hence T). Screen per experiment: |(d ln Φε/dE)(dT/dE)|·σ_R² ≪ δT.

Regime physics — what constrains the fit

⚑ Saturation & the curve of growth

As (n·d)·σ grows the dip deepens toward T≈0 (never exactly zero at finite optical depth) and — at fixed temperature — widens: the half-depth crossings slide into the Lorentzian wings. So the width does not encode temperature alone.

Both dips are at the same temperature. Raising areal density widens the dip and eventually saturates it — so FWHM ⇏ temperature. n·d and T are jointly encoded and must be fit together. "FWHM gives T without density" holds only optically-thin (τ₀ ≲ 0.3) with Γ and the resolution already known.

A saturated core is not pure background: its measured counts are background + resolution-leakage from neighboring energies + in-scatter + gammas + frame overlap. Useful as a model-assisted constraint, not a free zero-reference.

Design consequence: prefer resonances at τ₀ ≲ 0.3 for quantitative width/temperature; the dynamic-range limit is soft (statistics + background + nuclear-data uncertainty), and saturated resonances still give a lower bound on n·d and a background/resolution constraint.

Degeneracies — why fits are coupled

n·d ⊗ T — jointly encoded in every resonance (curve of growth); fit together, never read off depth vs width. "Pin one" is valid only at τ₀ ≲ 0.3.
resolution ⊗ T — both broaden; calibration determines resolution first, carried as a prior (not hard-pinned).
energy scale (L, t₀) ⊗ resonance position — a wrong axis mimics shifted resonances → anchored by the known calibrant.
n·d ⊗ normalization ⊗ background — dip depth vs overall scale vs baseline all trade off.
n ⊗ d — transmission sees only the product (areal density); volumetric needs geometry / tomography.

The instrument is carried as a correlated calibration prior or fit jointly — not hard-pinned. Hard-pinning L/t₀/θ_res reports the "instrument perfectly known" covariance C_cond; the honest one is C_marg = C_cond + J·Σ_cal·Jᵀ ⪰ C_cond. In a toy Monte-Carlo the pinned 1σ bar covered only 20.4% of trials vs the nominal 68.3% (≈4× too small; up to ~27× near the T–resolution degeneracy). Joint fitting is both honest and tighter, because sharp resonances re-measure the resolution and energy scale.
